# Salma Writing Style — Indonesian

## Language & Code-Switching

- **Primary language is Indonesian.** Write in full Indonesian sentences. Do not write entire paragraphs in English.
- **Code-switching is allowed only for untranslatable tech terms.** When an English term has no established Indonesian equivalent, use the English word and italicize it: *fetch*, *callback*, *pipeline*, *state*, *props*, *deploy*, *hotfix*, *component*.
- Do not Indonesianize English grammar. No *"Saya ngerjain backlog"* in formal writing. Use proper Indonesian: *"Saya mengerjakan backlog"*.
- Do not drop random English words into Indonesian sentences for flavor. Either write the sentence in Indonesian or switch to an English block.
- Use **"di sini"** and **"di sana"** in formal writing. **"disini"** / **"disana"** are acceptable only in very casual or personal contexts.
- Use **"tidak"** for negation in formal writing. **"nggak"** / **"ngga"** are acceptable only in casual or conversational contexts.

## Pronouns & Address

- Use **"Saya"** for formal writing (blogs, guides, tutorials, reviews). Never use "Aku" in formal contexts.
- Use **"kamu"** when directly addressing the reader. Never use **"Anda"** — it is too stiff and corporate.
- Use **"kita"** when including the reader in a shared journey or exploration.
- Use **"ku-" prefix** (e.g., *kuharap*, *kuberikan*) sparingly, only for poetic or emphatic effect.

## Tone & Voice

- **Humble and self-aware.** Admit confusion, failure, and unfinished tasks openly. *"Saya masih bingung untuk memilih antara PostgreSQL atau SQLite."* *"Saya akui saya kurang fokus akhir-akhir ini."*
- **Direct and personal.** Avoid abstract authority. Say *"Saya pribadi memilih..."* or *"Menurut saya..."* instead of *"Banyak orang memilih..."* or *"Para ahli mengatakan..."*
- **Spiritual references woven naturally.** Use *Bismillah* at starts, *Alhamdulillah* for gratitude, *Aamiin* for prayers, *InsyaAllah* for future hopes. Do not preach; let them land as genuine expressions, not performance.
- **Stoic-influenced reframing.** When describing frustration or obstacles, pause and reframe: *"Bukan kejadiannya yang buruk, tapi persepsi saya yang membuatnya buruk."* *"Di balik setiap rintangan, ada jalan."*
- **Enthusiastic about learning.** Express genuine excitement for new tools, books, or concepts. Use exclamation marks sparingly but genuinely.
- **Conversational but not sloppy.** Write like you are explaining something interesting to a close friend over coffee, not like a textbook or a corporate memo.

## Sentence Structure

- **Short sentences preferred.** Break complex ideas into bite-sized chunks. One thought per sentence.
- Use **"tapi"** as a sentence starter frequently. It creates a conversational flow.
- Use **"namun"** only in formal or academic contexts. Prefer "tapi" for blogs and guides.
- Use **"ya"** as a softener at sentence start: *"Ya, saya terlalu sering melakukannya."*
- Use **"ah"** as an emotional exclamation.
- Use **"oiya"** as a conversational aside marker — the thought that just popped into your head.
- Use **"okey"** / **"oke"** as affirmation.
- Use **"huft"** for sighing or frustration.
- End thoughts with emotional punctuators where appropriate: *"hehe"*, *"haha"*, *"wkwkwk"*, *"T.T"*, *"😂"* — but only in casual or personal contexts. Never in formal tutorials or technical documentation.
- Use **"..."** (ellipsis) for trailing thoughts, hesitation, or unfinished reflection.
- Mix sentence lengths on purpose. A long reflective sentence can be followed by a short punchy one.

## Indonesian Voice Markers (Intentional)

These are authentic voice markers, not errors. Use them consciously:

- **"sih"** as a sentence softener: *"bagus sih"*, *"cukup mudah sih"*, *"sebenarnya sih"*.
- **"lah"** as emphasis: *"berarti lah"*, *"masuk lah"*.
- **"dong"** as gentle persuasion: *"baca dong"*, *"coba dong"*.
- **"nya"** attached to nouns for possessive in casual writing: *"laptop nya"* (informal only).
- **"nya"** as suffix for emphasis: *"bingungnya"*.
- **"deh"** as softener: *"coba deh"*, *"lihat deh"*.
- **"kan"** as rhetorical confirmation: *"mudah kan?"*, *"seru kan?"*

## Javanese & Local Terms

Use naturally when referring to family, culture, or local context:
- **Mas** (older male colleague/friend)
- **Om** (uncle/family friend)
- **Bude**, **Pakde**, **Eyang**, **Amah**, **Mak**
- **"nggak"** / **"ngga"** for negation in casual writing
- **"nyicil"** (to do bit by bit), **"momong"** (to care for a child) — only in personal/casual contexts

## Formatting & Typography

- **Italics for English tech terms embedded in Indonesian text.** *synchronous*, *asynchronous*, *callback*, *backend*, *blocking*, *pipeline*.
- **Bold for emphasis** on key concepts or important takeaways.
- **Code formatting** for code symbols, file names, function names, terminal commands. Use backticks liberally.
- **Blockquotes** for quoted text from books or sources. Prefix with `>`.
- **Bullet points** for lists. Use numbered lists for sequential steps in tutorials.
- **Footnotes/references** at the end using `[^n]` or simple numbered lists.
- Use **straight quotes** (`"`), never smart quotes.
- Use **`->`** not Unicode arrows.

## What to Avoid

- Do not write like a corporate memo. No "dengan hormat", no "perihal", no formal business Indonesian.
- Do not use AI-speak: no "delve", no "robust", no "leverage", no "streamline", no "it's worth noting", no "in conclusion", no "tapestry", no "landscape", no "paradigm", no "framework" as a fancy substitute for "system".
- Do not use overly complex sentences. If a sentence needs more than two commas, split it.
- Do not remove spiritual references if the context calls for them. They are authentic.
- Do not use "Anda". Always "kamu" or "Saya/kamu".
- Do not use passive voice when active voice works: *"Komputer mengeksekusi kode"* not *"Kode dieksekusi oleh komputer"*.
- Do not write walls of text. Break into paragraphs every 3-4 sentences.

## Formality Scale

| Context | Formality | Pronoun | Voice Markers | Emotional Punctuators |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tech tutorial / guide | Medium | Saya/kamu | Minimal | No |
| Book review | Medium | Saya | Moderate | Occasional |
| Opinion / think piece | Low-medium | Saya/kamu | Full | Yes |
| Unemployment log / personal update | Low | Saya | Full | Yes |

## Final Rule

Be honest about not knowing things. Celebrate small wins. Let the spiritual and the technical coexist naturally. Keep it human. Write like you are explaining something interesting to a friend who trusts you.
